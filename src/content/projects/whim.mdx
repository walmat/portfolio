Whim lets you rent premium products by the month from the brands that build them. You pick it up
where you were already shopping — you choose Whim on a partner brand's own site, one monthly
price ships the device to your door, and from then on you can swap it, upgrade it, have it
replaced if it breaks, or request a quote and just keep it.

That shape decides most of what the frontend has to be. Whim isn't a store. There's no catalog to
browse and no cart to fill. It's a payment option that lives inside somebody else's checkout, and
then a place you manage what you're renting. Fragile is the company behind it. I was responsible
for the frontend.

## The site

The marketing site carries the whole proposition before anyone has agreed to anything. The hero
scatters real product cutouts — a projector, an espresso machine, a pool, a PC case, a license
plate — loosely around the headline, so the page reads as _stuff you've been eyeing_ rather than
as a grid of SKUs.

Below it the page settles into an editorial rhythm: three claims, alternating sides, each one
letting a photograph do the arguing instead of a feature list. Try for as little or as long as you
want. Swap, upgrade, or replace anytime. Keep what you love.

The scatter is the part that can't just be a background image. Every cutout has to hold its
relationship to the headline as the viewport changes, and on a phone there's no room for most of
them at all — the composition thins out and regroups above and below the text rather than around
it, while the alternating rows collapse into a single column of text-then-image.
